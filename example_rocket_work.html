Work Done by a Variable Force: Rocket Ascent

When a rocket burns through tons of propellants each second, its weight continuously drops. How definite integrals compute the exact energy (Joules) needed to reach orbit.

The Real-Life Problem

A rocket payload weighs $2,000\text{ kg}$ at launch. As it blasts straight up into space, it burns fuel at a constant rate, causing it to lose weight as it rises. Its total mass (in $\text{kg}$) at a height of $x$ meters is modeled by:

$$m(x) = 2,000 - 0.1x$$

Target Question:
How much work (in Joules) is required to lift the rocket from the ground ($x = 0$) to an altitude of $10,000\text{ m}$? (Assume standard earth gravity $g \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).

💡 The High Schooler's Key Intuition

In Middle School, you learned $\text{Work} = \text{Force} \times \text{Distance}$. But that formula only works when force stays constant! In the real world, rockets get lighter every second. The integral $\int F(x)\,dx$ is simply adding up millions of tiny $\Delta x$ force slices!

Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Here is how to solve this exact AP Calculus and physics examination problem with rigor:

Step 1: Formulate the Downward Gravitational Force Function $F(x)$

By Newton's Second Law, the gravitational downward weight that the rocket engine must counteract at height $x$ is:

$$F(x) = m(x) \cdot g = (2,000 - 0.1x) \times 9.8$$ $$F(x) = 19,600 - 0.98x \quad \text{Newtons (N)}$$

• At the launchpad ($x = 0$): $F(0) = 19,600\text{ N}$ ($19.6\text{ kN}$).
• At target altitude ($x = 10,000\text{ m}$): $F(10,000) = 19,600 - 9,800 = 9,800\text{ N}$ (exactly half its initial weight, having burned $1,000\text{ kg}$ of fuel!).

Step 2: Set Up the Riemann Sum and Definite Integral

Divide the $10,000\text{-meter}$ flight path into $n$ tiny height increments of length $\Delta x$. Over any small slice, the force is roughly constant. The work $\Delta W_i$ to raise the rocket through slice $i$ is:

$$\Delta W_i \approx F(x_i) \cdot \Delta x = (19,600 - 0.98x_i)\,\Delta x$$

Taking the limit as the slices shrink ($\Delta x \to 0$ and $n \to \infty$) gives the definite integral:

$$W = \int_{0}^{10,000} F(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{10,000} (19,600 - 0.98x)\,dx$$

Step 3: Evaluate Using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Find the anti-derivative with the power rule:

$$W = \left[ 19,600x - 0.98 \cdot \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{10,000} = \left[ 19,600x - 0.49x^2 \right]_{0}^{10,000}$$

Substitute the upper limit $x = 10,000$ and lower limit $x = 0$:

$$W = 19,600(10,000) - 0.49(10,000)^2$$ $$W = 196,000,000 - 0.49(100,000,000) = 196,000,000 - 49,000,000$$ $$W = 147,000,000\text{ Joules} = 147\text{ Megajoules (MJ)}$$
🚀 Engineering Insight: If the rocket did not burn fuel and remained at its full $2,000\text{ kg}$ weight the whole time, the work would have been $19,600 \times 10,000 = 196\text{ MJ}$. Burning fuel saved $49\text{ MJ}$ of energy—the exact reason modern aerospace uses multi-stage liquid propellant rockets!
