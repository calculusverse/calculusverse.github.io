Total Distance from Speed: Area Accumulation

If your speedometer is constantly changing, simple multiplication $(d = v \times t)$ fails. Here is how definite integration calculates the exact distance traveled.

The Real-Life Problem

A high-speed express train travels between two metropolitan hubs over a $4\text{-hour}$ journey. Due to terrain and scheduled throttle adjustments, its velocity $v(t)$ (in $\text{km/h}$) varies over time $t$ (in hours) according to:

$$v(t) = -15t^2 + 60t + 80 \quad (0 \le t \le 4\text{ hours})$$

Target Question:
How many total kilometers does the train travel over the entire $4\text{-hour}$ trip?

🚅 Speed $v(t)$ & Shaded Area = Distance
🚅
Time Elapsed: t = 4.0 hr
Current Speed: 80 km/h
Total Distance: 480.0 km

Trip Timeline

Travel Time ($T$) 4.0 hr
Definite Integral Area $\int_0^T v(t)\,dt$
$$\int_0^{4.0} v(t)\,dt = 480.0\text{ km}$$
Notice how every single vertical slice has units of $\text{km/h} \times \text{hours} = \text{kilometers}$! Adding them together produces total accumulated distance.

Step-by-Step Calculus Solution

Step 1: Set Up the Distance Integral

Since distance is the accumulation of velocity over time:

$$D = \int_0^4 v(t)\,dt = \int_0^4 \left(-15t^2 + 60t + 80\right)\,dt$$

Step 2: Find the Antiderivative

Integrate each term using the reverse power rule $\int t^n\,dt = \frac{t^{n+1}}{n+1}$:

$$\int (-15t^2 + 60t + 80)\,dt = -15\left(\frac{t^3}{3}\right) + 60\left(\frac{t^2}{2}\right) + 80t = -5t^3 + 30t^2 + 80t$$

Step 3: Evaluate at the Boundaries $[0, 4]$

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:

$$D = \left[-5(4)^3 + 30(4)^2 + 80(4)\right] - \left[0\right]$$

Computing the arithmetic:

$$D = -5(64) + 30(16) + 320 = -320 + 480 + 320 = \mathbf{480\text{ km}}$$

The train traveled an exact distance of 480 kilometers over the 4 hours!
