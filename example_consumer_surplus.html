Consumer Surplus: The Rideshare Pricing Secret

How definite integrals measure the collective happiness and financial value saved by millions of everyday app users who were willing to pay higher fares.

The Real-Life Problem

A ride-sharing app analyzes local market data and models its weekly ride demand price by the linear function:

$$p(x) = 40 - 0.05x$$

where $p$ is the price per ride (in dollars), and $x$ is the quantity of rides requested (in thousands). Market equilibrium stabilizes at a flat rate of $15 per ride ($p^* = 15$).

Target Question:
What is the total Consumer Surplus? (The total dollar savings gained by customers who would have gladly paid more than $15 for urgent rides).

Pricing Scenarios:
💡 Real-World Story: What is Consumer Surplus?

Suppose you are rushing to the airport and willing to pay $35 for an urgent ride. When you open the app and see the ride only costs $15, you just saved $20! If you add up the savings of all 500,000 riders, that shaded green area is the total $6,250,000 kept in consumers' pockets!

Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Here is how economists and mathematicians calculate Consumer Surplus using integral calculus:

Step 1: Determine Equilibrium Quantity $x^*$

Set the demand price equation equal to the equilibrium market price $p^* = 15$:

$$p(x) = p^*$$ $$40 - 0.05x = 15$$ $$0.05x = 40 - 15 = 25$$ $$x^* = \frac{25}{0.05} = 500\text{ thousand rides (500,000 total rides)}$$

This means when price stabilizes at $15, exactly 500,000 passengers choose to book rides that week.

Step 2: Set Up the Consumer Surplus Integral

Consumer Surplus represents the area beneath the demand curve $p(x)$ and above the equilibrium horizontal price line $p^*$:

$$CS = \int_{0}^{x^*} [p(x) - p^*]\,dx$$ $$CS = \int_{0}^{500} [(40 - 0.05x) - 15]\,dx = \int_{0}^{500} (25 - 0.05x)\,dx$$

Step 3: Evaluate Using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Integrate with respect to $x$:

$$CS = \left[ 25x - 0.05 \cdot \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{500} = \left[ 25x - 0.025x^2 \right]_{0}^{500}$$

Substitute limits:

$$CS = 25(500) - 0.025(500)^2$$ $$CS = 12,500 - 0.025(250,000) = 12,500 - 6,250 = 6,250\text{ thousand dollars}$$ $$CS = \$6,250,000\text{ (6.25 Million Dollars!)}$$

Geometric Double-Check: Triangle Area Formula

Because the demand curve is linear, the surplus region is an exact right-angled triangle:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Base} \times \text{Height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 500 \times (40 - 15) = \frac{1}{2} \times 500 \times 25 = 6,250\text{ thousand dollars}$$

Both calculus integration and elementary geometry match to the exact dollar, proving the beauty and harmony of mathematics!
