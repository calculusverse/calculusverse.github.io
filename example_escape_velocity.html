Escape Velocity & Related Rates: Freefall in Space

How the Chain Rule connects velocity dependent on position with acceleration dependent on time: $\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dr} \cdot \frac{dr}{dt}$.

The Real-Life Problem

An astronaut drops a heavy tool out of a space capsule hovering $2,000\text{ km}$ ($2 \times 10^6\text{ meters}$) above a small celestial planetoid. As it falls straight down, the object accelerates under planetary gravity. Newton’s Law of Gravitation dictates that its downward velocity $v$ at a radial distance $r$ from the planet's center is:

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$$

If the gravitational constant product $2GM \approx 8 \times 10^{12}\text{ m}^3\text{/s}^2$, how fast is the tool accelerating at the exact moment it passes a point $1,500\text{ km}$ ($1.5 \times 10^6\text{ meters}$) from the center?

💡 Why Related Rates are So Magical

$v$ is given as a formula of distance $r$, not time $t$! We don't need a stopwatch to find acceleration: the Chain Rule connects how fast speed changes across space with how fast space changes across time: $$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt}$$

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

Two independent ways to find the acceleration—both arriving at Newton's universal inverse-square law:

Method 1: The Chain Rule (Related Rates)

Acceleration is defined as the time rate of change of velocity: $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$. By the Chain Rule:

$$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dr} \cdot \frac{dr}{dt}$$

Now we calculate each piece individually:

Piece A: Find $\frac{dv}{dr}$
Rewrite the velocity function in exponential form: $v = (2GM)^{1/2} \cdot r^{-1/2}$. Differentiating with respect to $r$:

$$\frac{dv}{dr} = (2GM)^{1/2} \left( -\frac{1}{2} r^{-3/2} \right) = -\frac{\sqrt{2GM}}{2 r^{3/2}}$$

Piece B: Find $\frac{dr}{dt}$
Since the object is falling inward toward the center, its radial distance $r$ is decreasing at exactly the speed $v$:

$$\frac{dr}{dt} = -v = -\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$$

Piece C: Multiply the two rates

$$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \left( -\frac{\sqrt{2GM}}{2 r^{3/2}} \right) \cdot \left( -\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}} \right) = \frac{2GM}{2 r^2} = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

Method 2: Implicit Differentiation (Faster Alternative)

Square both sides of $v = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$ to eliminate the square root:

$$v^2 = \frac{2GM}{r}$$

Differentiate both sides with respect to time $t$:

$$\frac{d}{dt}[v^2] = \frac{d}{dt}\left[ 2GM \cdot r^{-1} \right]$$ $$2v \frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{2GM}{r^2} \cdot \frac{dr}{dt}$$

Since the downward velocity is $v$, $\frac{dr}{dt} = -v$. Substituting:

$$2v \frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{2GM}{r^2}(-v) = \frac{2GM}{r^2} v$$

Divide both sides by $2v$:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = a = \frac{2GM}{2r^2} = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

Step 3: Calculating Numerical Acceleration at $r = 1,500\text{ km}$

Given $2GM = 8 \times 10^{12}\text{ m}^3\text{/s}^2$ and $r = 1,500\text{ km} = 1.5 \times 10^6\text{ m}$:

$$a = \frac{8 \times 10^{12}}{2 \times (1.5 \times 10^6)^2} = \frac{8 \times 10^{12}}{2 \times (2.25 \times 10^{12})} = \frac{8}{4.5} = \frac{16}{9} \approx 1.78\text{ m/s}^2$$

Answer: At the exact moment the tool passes $1,500\text{ km}$ from the center, it is accelerating downward at precisely $\frac{16}{9} \approx 1.78\text{ m/s}^2$. Notice that the negative signs cancel out, confirming that downward acceleration is pulling the tool faster toward the planet!
