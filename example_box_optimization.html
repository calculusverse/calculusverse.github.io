Maximizing Box Volume: Packaging Optimization

How industrial packaging engineers use derivatives to manufacture shipping boxes holding maximum volume using the minimum amount of material.

The Real-Life Problem

A factory produces open-top storage containers from flat rectangular sheets of cardboard measuring $20\text{ cm}$ in length and $12\text{ cm}$ in width. To fold the box, equal square corners of side length $x\text{ cm}$ are cut out from all four corners, and the remaining flaps are folded upwards.

$$V(x) = x(20 - 2x)(12 - 2x)$$

Target Question:
What size square corner $x$ should be cut out to maximize the interior volume of the resulting box?

Step-by-Step Calculus Solution

Step 1: Express the Volume Function

From the original sheet of $20 \times 12\text{ cm}$, removing corners of length $x$ leaves:

$$\text{Height} = x, \quad \text{Length} = 20 - 2x, \quad \text{Width} = 12 - 2x$$

Multiplying length $\times$ width $\times$ height gives the polynomial:

$$V(x) = x(20 - 2x)(12 - 2x) = 4x^3 - 64x^2 + 240x$$

Step 2: Differentiate and Find Critical Points ($V'(x) = 0$)

To find local maximums or minimums, we find where the tangent slope is zero:

$$V'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}[4x^3 - 64x^2 + 240x] = 12x^2 - 128x + 240$$

Set $V'(x) = 0$ and divide the entire equation by $4$:

$$3x^2 - 32x + 60 = 0$$

Factor the quadratic:

$$(3x - 10)(x - 6) = 0 \implies x = \frac{10}{3} \approx 3.333\text{ cm} \quad \text{or} \quad x = 6\text{ cm}$$

Step 3: Test and Conclude

If $x = 6$, the width becomes $12 - 2(6) = 0\text{ cm}$, producing a zero-volume line. Thus, $x = \frac{10}{3}\text{ cm}$ is our valid solution.
Substituting $x = \frac{10}{3}$ back into $V(x)$:

$$V_{\text{max}} = \left(\frac{10}{3}\right)\left(20 - \frac{20}{3}\right)\left(12 - \frac{20}{3}\right) = \frac{6400}{27} \approx \mathbf{237.04\text{ cm}^3}$$
